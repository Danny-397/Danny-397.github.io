The Lucky Seed

Nearly every impressive claim about AI in finance is a single training run, and a single training run is not evidence of anything.

I trained the same agent five times with nothing different but the random seed. One run would have made a spectacular headline. The other four are why I don't believe the headline.

Basis — single favorable run +275% vs. +19% benchmark · five seeds, crypto −2.7% [−31%, +27%], p ≈ 0.97 · stocks −19% [−29%, −7%] vs. buy-and-hold +260%, p ≈ 0.002

More Compute Was Not the Answer

“Scale it up” is the reflex answer to a model that won't learn. I scaled mine ten-fold and it bought me fourteen Elo points.

The interesting part isn't that it failed. It's what the failure mode told me: 92% of self-play games ended non-decisively, which means the value head was being trained almost entirely on draws. The bottleneck was the signal, not the hardware.

Basis — 100 games, −21 Elo vs. random · 1,000 games (10×), −7 Elo, 92% non-decisive · material-assisted, +28 Elo · alpha-beta control beat random 10–0

I Built the Instrument That Indicts My Own Results

If you search hard enough over strategies, you will find one that worked. That is a property of searching, not of the strategy.

So I implemented the tests that measure it — and pointed them at my own backtests first.

Basis — 42-variant grid under CPCV on SPY · probability of backtest overfitting 63% · median in-sample Sharpe surviving out-of-sample ~38% · ML forecaster test AUC ≈0.51
