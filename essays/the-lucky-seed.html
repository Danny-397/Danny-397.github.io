Essay · Reinforcement learning

The Lucky Seed

I trained the same trading agent five times, changing nothing but the random seed. One of those runs returned 275%. That run is the reason I no longer believe results like it — including, for a while, my own.

Draft Evidence: RL-Trader Code & data public

How to use this page

The structure, the figure and every number are in place — all of it comes from your own runs. What's missing is the argument, which has to be yours: an admissions reader can tell the difference between someone reporting a result and someone thinking about one, and the whole point of this page is to show the second thing.

Each section below has a note saying what that section must accomplish and the trap to avoid. Write the prose, then delete the note. When every dashed box is gone, the essay is done.

01The claim you see everywhere

What this section must do

  • Open with the specific belief you're going to attack — that machine learning can find durable edge in markets, stated the way its advocates state it.
  • Be fair to it. Steelman it. If you strawman here, everything after is worthless.
  • Trap: don't open with “AI is everywhere these days.” Open with a specific claim a specific kind of person makes.
  • Your unfair advantage: you believed this. Say so, and say what made you believe it.

02What I built to test it

What this section must do

  • Establish that the test was honest before you knew the answer — chronological splits, scalers fit on training data only, transaction costs, a real benchmark. Pre-committing to the method is what separates this from data mining.
  • Keep it short. Two paragraphs. The method is in the repo; this is an essay.
  • Trap: don't list every hyperparameter. Nobody is grading your implementation here.
  • Worth one line: you wrote PPO from scratch rather than importing it, which is why you could see what it was doing.

03The run that would have fooled me

What this section must do

  • Tell it honestly as the moment it happened: +275% against a +19% benchmark. Describe what you felt, briefly — this is the one place emotion belongs.
  • Then the pivot: what made you run it again instead of writing it up.
  • This is the hinge of the essay. The interesting person isn't the one who got 275%. It's the one who distrusted it.

04What five runs showed

What this section must do

  • Walk the reader through the figure in plain English. Assume they don't know what a confidence interval is and teach it in one sentence without condescending.
  • Land the asymmetry: crypto is inconclusive, stocks is significantly worse than doing nothing. Those are different failures and you should say why.
  • Trap: don't say “the model failed.” The model did exactly what it was optimized to do. The evaluation was what had been failing.

05Why one run looks so much better than five

What this section must do

  • Explain the mechanism, not just the observation. Why is deep RL specifically this high-variance? Initialization, exploration noise, the feedback loop where an early lucky trajectory shapes everything the agent sees next.
  • Connect to your domain-randomization result: an agent trained on a single price path memorizes it; training on a fresh synthetic path per episode closed the gap by two to three orders of magnitude. That's your own evidence for what's actually going wrong.
  • Cite Henderson et al.1 here — you independently reproduced their finding in a domain they didn't test.
  • Trap: this is the section most likely to turn into a textbook. Keep your own voice.

06The counterargument I have to beat

What this section must do

  • State the strongest objection to your own essay and answer it. Candidates: “you're a student with limited compute — real firms have more data and better features, so your null result says nothing about theirs.” That objection is partly correct. Say which part.
  • Your honest answer is probably about what a null result can and cannot establish. That distinction is the most sophisticated thing in the essay — don't skip it to look more certain than you are.
  • Including this section is most of its value. Almost nobody argues against themselves; readers notice.

07What I do differently now

What this section must do

  • Concrete changes to your own practice, not a moral. You built AlphaGlyph's overfitting tests after this — and it reports 63% probability of backtest overfitting on your own strategy grid. That's the strongest possible ending: you built the instrument that convicts you.
  • End on the standard you now hold yourself to, in one sentence, without grandeur.
  • Trap: no “and that's what I learned about perseverance.” Let the work carry it.

Notes

  1. Henderson, P., Islam, R., Bachman, P., Pineau, J., Precup, D., & Meger, D. “Deep Reinforcement Learning That Matters.” AAAI, 2018 — the paper establishing that single-run reinforcement learning evaluations are unreliable. Verify the citation against the published version before you post this.
